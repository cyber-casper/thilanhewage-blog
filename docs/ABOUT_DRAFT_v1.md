# About — Draft v1 (thilanhewage.com)

**Status:** Draft for Casper's review. Sourced from two independent AI accounts (Claude
Cowork/Code, ChatGPT Codex) of working with him — Grok's answer is excluded, it didn't have

---

I spent a long time with ideas I couldn't get out of my head and into the world. Not because
the ideas were bad — because turning "I have an idea" into something real took more hours,
more skills, and more patience than I had to spend alone. That gap is most of why this site
exists as late as it does.

What changed is AI. Not as a buzzword — as leverage. I run a small, self-hosted infrastructure
practice: agents I've built and deployed myself (Hermes, Odysseus), containerized with Docker,
Caddy, and Dockge on my own VPS, debugged live until they passed health checks rather than
patched around. I'm building an all-in-one workplace app on Claude Cowork, wiring up MCP
servers and working through the same kind of infrastructure decisions — cloud-routed versus
local, WSL2 versus native Linux — that used to be the wall between an idea and a shipped thing.
This site is the plainest proof of that: a personal blog, live, with its own Notion-backed
content pipeline, built and deployed end to end in a matter of days.

HexOrbit is where the same instinct runs at studio scale. It's not one product — it's Wealth
Tracker (a personal finance app, now on v2), FlowSync (a Pomodoro app with a mobile port in
progress), a research digest pipeline, and a handful of smaller tools, all running on
infrastructure I operate myself. I write a spec before I build, an ADR when a decision has real
tradeoffs, and a dev log after — even when I'm the only one who has to read it. That's not
process for its own sake. It's a habit built to outlast being a team of one.

I debug by isolating problems, not guessing at them. I'll reverse a tooling decision the moment
it stops working rather than defend it because I already committed to it. And I'd rather hear
"I don't know" than a plausible-sounding answer — from a person or from an AI — which is part
of why I push the tools I use to be specific instead of complimentary.

None of this — the agents, the workplace app, this blog — would exist on this timeline without
AI doing the work that used to be the bottleneck between me and finishing something. I think
it's time more of us said that plainly instead of downplaying it. AI didn't hand me my ideas.
It gave me a way to actually build them.

I'm Casper — currently working toward Azure AZ-900 and AZ-104, building HexOrbit, and writing
here about what that actually looks like in practice.
